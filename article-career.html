How to Prepare Your First Internship CV

Written by Marcus Vance, Graduate Careers Strategist

The hardest resume to write in your entire professional career is your very first one. You stare at a blank word document with a gnawing sense of self-doubt: “Every internship application asks for 1 to 2 years of relevant work experience, but I have only ever been a full-time student. What on earth am I supposed to write?”

Here is the insider truth that university career fairs rarely articulate: corporate recruiters hiring summer interns do not expect a decade of corporate employment. What they are looking for is evidence of curiosity, intellectual capability, technical craft, and disciplined execution.

Your university degree is packed with valuable artifacts: laboratory experiments, capstone software applications, statistical research papers, hackathon projects, and student society leadership. When formatted through the right lens, these experiences demonstrate immense professional potential. Below is our complete architectural blueprint for engineering a winning student CV.

1. Contact Information: Keep It Clean and Professional

Do not waste valuable top-of-page vertical space with physical street addresses, date of birth, or decorative graphic bars. Keep your header strictly functional:

  • Full Name: Prominent 16–18pt bold serif or sans-serif heading.
  • Location: City, Country or State (e.g., “Boston, MA” or “London, UK”).
  • Professional Email: Use your official university email (name@university.edu) or a clean personal address (firstname.lastname@email.com). Never use frivolous teenage handles.
  • Digital Anchors: Hyperlink your LinkedIn profile, GitHub repository, or personal portfolio website. Ensure the links actually work!

2. Education Section: Lead with Academic Rigor

As a student or recent graduate, your Education section belongs at the top of your CV—directly below your contact header. Once you have accumulated two years of full-time industry experience, education drops to the bottom, but right now, it is your primary qualification.

  • Degree Title & Major: e.g., B.Sc. in Computer Science & Mathematics (Honours).
  • Institution & Expected Graduation: e.g., University of Edinburgh • Expected June 2027.
  • Academic Performance (GPA/Classification): Include your GPA if it is 3.5/4.0 or above, or if you hold a First Class / Upper Second standing. If your GPA is lower, omit it and highlight your project achievements instead.
  • Relevant Coursework: List 4 to 6 upper-level modules directly relevant to the internship role (e.g., Data Structures & Algorithms, Database Systems, Linear Algebra, Machine Learning).

3. Academic & Personal Projects: The Heart of Your CV

If you have no corporate employment history, your Projects section is where you win the interview. Treat substantial academic assignments, capstone dissertations, and weekend coding projects with the exact same prestige as an employer would treat a professional engagement.

⚡ The STAR Formula for Project Bullets

Never write passive descriptions like: “Responsible for making a web app for class.” That tells the recruiter nothing about your contribution. Instead, use the STAR Method (Situation, Task, Action, Result):

  • Formula: [Strong Action Verb] + [Context & Technical Tool] + [Quantified Outcome or Impact].
  • Example 1: “Engineered a full-stack student course scheduler using Python and PostgreSQL, reducing schedule conflict resolution time by 40% for 850 campus beta testers.”
  • Example 2: “Analyzed a dataset of 50,000 meteorological records in R to construct a predictive rainfall model, achieving 91% classification accuracy in final semester peer review.”

4. Technical and Professional Skills Categorization

Do not list “Microsoft Word” or use subjective visual percentage bars (e.g., “Python: 85%” - what does 85% of Python even mean?). Group your proficiencies into clean, objective categories:

  • Programming Languages: Python, Java, C++, TypeScript, SQL, Bash.
  • Frameworks & Libraries: React, Express, Pandas, NumPy, Tailwind CSS.
  • Developer Tools & Platforms: Git, GitHub, Linux, Docker, VS Code, Figma.
  • Soft & Collaborative Competencies: Technical Writing, Agile Scrum, Team Leadership, Peer Code Review.

5. Leadership, Societies & Extracurriculars

Employers value well-rounded candidates who demonstrate initiative outside the lecture hall. Were you the treasurer of the campus robotics society? Did you organize a 200-person collegiate hackathon? Did you volunteer as a peer calculus tutor?

Highlighting leadership roles proves to hiring managers that you possess communication stamina, conflict resolution skills, and accountability.

6. Seven Common Student CV Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Exceeding One Page: As an undergraduate, your CV must strictly be one single page. Recruiters spend an average of 7 seconds on initial screening; multi-page student resumes read as unfocused.
  2. Passive Duty Descriptions: Avoid phrases like “Assisted with...”, “Worked on...”, or “Duties included...”. Always lead with active, impact verbs: Engineered, Formulated, Streamlined, Synthesized, Spearheaded.
  3. Unclickable or Dead Links: If you list your GitHub or portfolio URL, verify that every repository is public and includes a clean README.md with screenshot previews.
  4. Inconsistent Formatting & Fonts: Mixing three different font faces, random margins, or uneven bullet alignments makes your application look careless.
  5. Sending Word Documents: Always export and submit your final application as a clean, standardized PDF file named Firstname_Lastname_CV.pdf. Word files frequently render misaligned across different operating systems.
  6. Vague Skill Claims: Never write “Proficient in all programming languages.” Be honest and specific about tools you have genuinely used to build working software.
  7. Spelling and Grammatical Errors: A single typo in an educational CV signals low attention to detail. Proofread your document three times aloud before submitting.
“A CV is not an exhaustive autobiography of everything you have ever done. It is a targeted promotional brochure demonstrating why you will succeed in a specific role.” — Marcus Vance, Graduate Careers Strategist

Sample Student CV Structure

Below is an example of an ATS-optimized, single-column CV architecture modeled for an undergraduate student applying for technical or analytical internships:

ALEX MORGAN

Boston, MA · alex.morgan@college.edu · (555) 019-2834 · linkedin.com/in/alexmorgan-student · github.com/alexmorgan-dev

Education

University of Massachusetts · B.S. in Computer Science Expected May 2027

GPA: 3.86 / 4.0 · Dean’s List (All Semesters) · Chancellor’s Academic Scholar

Relevant Modules: Algorithms & Data Structures, Database Systems, Computer Systems, Software Engineering, Linear Algebra.

Technical & Academic Projects

Campus Ride-Share Web Portal · Lead Developer Sep 2026 – Dec 2026
  • Engineered a responsive student ride-matching web application utilizing Python, Flask, and SQLite.
  • Implemented an optimized route-clustering algorithm that paired students with matching commute schedules.
  • Deployed application to 1,200+ campus peers during orientation, facilitating over 400 verified shared rides.
Autonomous Sensor Bot · Embedded Systems Lab Feb 2026 – May 2026
  • Constructed and programmed a dual-microcontroller autonomous navigation robot using C++ and Arduino.
  • Calibrated ultrasonic and infrared distance sensors to achieve real-time obstacle avoidance with sub-15ms response latency.
  • Awarded 1st Place out of 28 student engineering groups in the semester Capstone Robotics Showcase.

Leadership & Campus Involvement

University Computing Society · Workshops Director Sep 2025 – Present
  • Organized 8 hands-on technical workshops covering Git workflows and Python basics for 150+ freshman attendees.
  • Partnered with campus alumni to host an annual tech career panel, connecting 80 students with industry mentors.

Technical Skills

Languages: Python, C++, Java, SQL, HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript.

Developer Tools: Git, GitHub, Linux Shell, VS Code, Postman, Docker, Figma.

Conclusion: Submit Early and Iterate

The secret to securing competitive internships is early application volume combined with thoughtful tailoring. Do not wait until spring to start applying; premier internship programs open as early as August and September. Build your one-page CV today, seek feedback from your university career center or peer mentors, and iterate with confidence.

Marcus Vance

Graduate Careers Strategist and former collegiate recruiting coordinator. He has audited over 2,500 student CVs and leads career accelerator workshops across universities in the UK and North America.
