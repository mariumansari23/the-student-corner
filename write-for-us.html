Student Contributors

Write for The Student Corner

Are you passionate about study hacks, cognitive psychology, campus life navigation, or internship hunting? Join our community of student contributors and amplify your voice across university campuses.

Why Write for Our Publication?

At The Student Corner, we believe the most credible, empathetic advice for undergraduates comes directly from fellow students who are actively navigating collegiate life. Writing for our publication offers tangible benefits:

  • Build a Professional Writing Portfolio: Showcase your analytical clarity and communication skills to future employers and graduate school admissions committees.
  • Receive Dedicated Editorial Mentorship: Work alongside our student editors to polish your rhetoric, typographic structuring, and storytelling.
  • Help Thousands of Fellow Students: Share the hard-won study systems, schedule templates, or interview insights that you wish someone had shared with you during freshman orientation.
  • Earn an Official Author Byline: Receive a permanent author profile, headshot bio, and verified link back to your personal portfolio or LinkedIn profile.

Accepted Topics & Themes

We accept pitches and original drafts across six core areas:

📚 What We Love to Publish

  • Study Science in Action: Detailed case studies of applying active recall, Cornell notes, or interleaving to specific demanding majors (e.g., Organic Chemistry, Engineering, Law, History).
  • Time Management & Calendars: Real-world breakdowns of weekly routines, commuter balance, or working 20 hours a week while maintaining a high GPA.
  • Campus Lifestyle & Community: Making friends in large lecture cohorts, roommate conflict resolution, mental health strategies, and navigating collegiate independence.
  • Internship Hunting & Career Prep: Candid recruiting retrospectives, CV tear-downs, portfolio guides, and technical or case-study interview preparation.
  • Tools & Learning Repositories: Objective evaluations of open-source software, markdown note-taking systems, and free academic resources.

Editorial Standards & Formatting Guidelines

Before preparing your pitch, review our standard publishing requirements:

  1. Word Count: Articles should typically run between 800 and 1,500 words. We prioritize depth, actionable utility, and clarity over superficial summaries.
  2. Evidence-Backed Voice: Whenever you make a factual claim about memory, productivity, or learning methods, cite verifiable sources (e.g., cognitive psychology studies, university guidelines).
  3. Clear Structural Hierarchy: Use descriptive headings (<h2>, <h3>), concise bulleted lists, and practical examples to make your writing easy to scan and digest.
  4. Strict Originality Policy: All submissions must be 100% original work authored by you. We maintain zero tolerance for plagiarism, recycled content, or work submitted without the original author’s permission. Every submission is vetted by human editors.
